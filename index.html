A Note from Gerald

I spent 27 years working in the pharmaceutical industry, and I have spent much of my life as a caregiver.

I have seen the healthcare system from both sides: the corporate side and the family side. I have sat in waiting rooms, argued with insurance companies, and held a loved one's hand through surgery. I have also seen how easily things fall through the cracks when you are exhausted, scared, and trying to make sense of medical jargon.

I wrote Surviving the U.S. Healthcare System because I saw too many people walk into appointments unprepared and walk out feeling confused or dismissed. The system assumes you already know the rules. Most of us do not.

You do not have to be a medical professional to advocate for yourself or someone you love. You just need a playbook. I hope this book helps you walk into your next appointment prepared instead of vulnerable.

— Gerald Lynn, C.M.R.

Who This Book Is For

If any of these situations sound familiar, this playbook was written with you in mind.

The Caregiver

You are managing appointments, medications, and insurance calls for someone you love. You are the primary advocate for an aging parent or a sick relative. You need a clear framework so nothing falls through the cracks.

The Patient

You have just received news that changes everything. You need to know what questions to ask, what records to gather, and how to build a care team you can trust when you are feeling vulnerable.

The Planner

You are healthy right now, but you want to be ready. You know that a medical emergency can happen to anyone. This book helps you organize your information and prepare before a crisis strikes.

What Can Go Wrong

The book pulls back the curtain on what really happens, and how to avoid the most common pitfalls.

Misdiagnoses

Learn how miscommunications between specialists and primary care doctors happen, and what questions to ask to ensure your symptoms are fully understood.

See Chapter 2

Medication Errors

Potentially fatal medication errors occur more often than you think. Discover how to track your prescriptions and verify dosages before you take them.

See Chapter 3

Unforeseen Drug Interactions

When multiple doctors prescribe different medications, dangerous interactions can slip through. Learn how to keep a master medication list and who to share it with.

See Chapter 4

Insurance Denials

An insurance denial can derail your recovery. Learn how to navigate the appeals process, understand your coverage, and fight back when a claim is rejected.

See Chapter 5

Discharge Failures

Leaving the hospital is a critical transition. If you are not ready to go home, or if you do not understand your discharge instructions, the risk of readmission spikes.

See Chapter 6

Care Transitions

Moving from a hospital to a skilled nursing facility or home care can be confusing. Understand the options available when you or a loved one cannot live alone.

See Chapter 7
